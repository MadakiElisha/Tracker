# Sim validation runbook (8 pass/fail checks)

Goal: close the loop in Gazebo + ArduPilot SITL and score it with `analyze_run.py`.
Nothing here has been run on real Gazebo yet; the offline model (`tests/closed_loop.py`) is not a substitute.

## Setup
1. `python3 sim/add_camera_to_iris.py ~/ardupilot_gazebo` (adds `/drone/camera`; `--revert` undoes it).
2. `sudo apt install python3-gz-transport13 python3-gz-msgs10`.
3. Start Gazebo with `sim/worlds/tracker_world.sdf`, SITL, and `sim/move_target.py` (see `sim/launch_sim.sh`).
4. Confirm images flow: `gz topic -e -t /drone/camera | head`.
5. Run: `python3 tracker.py --config config.yaml --source gz:/drone/camera --connect 127.0.0.1:14550` (`--help` lists all flags). Set `gcs.token`.
6. Score: `python3 analyze_run.py logs/runs/<run>.csv --follow-seconds 300 --soak-seconds 1800` (exit 1 on FAIL; INCONCLUSIVE = case not exercised).

## Checks
| # | Check | How to provoke | Pass |
|---|-------|----------------|------|
| 1 | Follow | Target walks/drives 5 min | centred >=90%, range within +-20% >=80%, yaw flips <=10/10 s |
| 2 | No creep | Lock a STATIONARY target (parked car / standing person), hold ~60 s | no drift commands; run `analyze_run.py FILE --locks` to find the window, then `--rest-window START END` |
| 3 | Lock loss | Move target out of view | lock drops, drone hovers |
| 4 | Occlusion | Pass target behind obstacle | lock/ID recovered or cleanly dropped |
| 5 | Failsafes | Kill video; kill MAVLink; low battery | HOVER, then RTL per `safety:` |
| 6 | Pilot override | Change mode away from GUIDED | tracker releases control |
| 7 | Soak | 30 min run | no crash/leak, error counters flat |
| 8 | Logs | after every run | CSV complete, analyzer runs |

Record results per run; tune `pid:` in `config.yaml` only between runs.

## Speed use cases and mission profiles

`config.yaml` has a `profiles:` section; select one with `python3 tracker.py --profile vehicle ...`.

| Profile | Cap | Altitude | Targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| pedestrian | 4 m/s | 6 m | walking, jogging |
| runner | 10 m/s | 6 m | sprinters |
| vehicle | 16 m/s | 10 m | cars, vans, buses |
| motorcycle | 20 m/s | 8 m | motorbikes, cyclists |

Offline model (`python3 tests/usecases.py [--gimbal off]`, also run by pytest):
- **Fixed camera (no gimbal): 10 of 12 pass.** Fails: stop-and-go traffic (target visible 96% of the
  time, just under the 98% bar: hard stops flip the lean) and the 28 m/s highway car.
- **With a gimbal: 11 of 12 pass.** Only the highway car fails: it exceeds the modelled 20 m/s airframe.

**What made the fixed camera work.** The drone leans 30-45 deg at speed and the camera leans with it.
The controller now (a) reads tilt from MAVLink ATTITUDE and corrects the vertical image error, and
(b) uses that corrected error in the forward-speed gate. Before (b), each lean closed the gate, which
cut speed, which flipped the lean: a 4-16 m/s oscillation that threw the target out of frame
(fixed camera passed 2 of 12; now 10 of 12).

**Limits.** This is a model, not Gazebo and not a flight. Not yet validated in Gazebo or on hardware.
A 10-15 inch frame leans harder than the model's airframe, so expect less margin; a gimbal, or a
camera mounted with extra up-tilt, is the next step if real flights lose the target. No obstacle avoidance.

**Target speeds in Gazebo.** `python3 sim/move_target.py --person-speed 1.4` (walk, default),
`--person-speed 3` (jog), `--vehicle-speed 8`. Speeds are wall-clock exact. The old fixed circle
moved the person at 3.1 m/s, a fast jog, not a walk.

## High-speed highway scenario (2 km road, sprinter 10 m/s, car 20 m/s)

All of this is **opt-in**: the original small world, `ardupilot_tracker.param` and `move_target.py` are
unchanged, and `sim/launch_sim.sh ardupilot` still starts exactly what it did before.

| What | File |
|---|---|
| 2 km, 4-lane road world (still named `tracker_world`) | `sim/worlds/highway_world.sdf` (generated by `sim/make_highway_world.py`) |
| Target mover (car / runner, ramp-up, optional lane changes) | `sim/move_highway.py` |
| Faster SITL limits (`WPNAV_SPEED` 25 m/s, 6 m/s², 60° lean) | `sim/params/ardupilot_fast.param` |
| Stronger-thrust quad (opt-in, experimental) | `sim/make_fast_quad.py` |
| Tracker profiles | `highway_runner` (cap 14 m/s), `highway` (cap 24 m/s) |

**Important:** the original SITL params cap guided-mode speed at 5 m/s (`WPNAV_SPEED,500`), so a
10-20 m/s chase is impossible without `ardupilot_fast.param`. `launch_sim.sh ... highway` merges both files.

### Run it (stock Iris first)

Terminal 1: `cd ~/Tracker && sim/launch_sim.sh ardupilot highway`
Terminal 2: `python3 sim/move_highway.py --target car --car-speed 20 --manual-start`  (car waits; press Enter after you lock it)
Terminal 3: `python3 tracker.py --source gz:/drone/camera --profile highway`  (press T, click the car, then press Enter in terminal 2)

The targets stand still for `--start-delay` seconds so you can take off and lock. Runner:
`--target runner --runner-speed 10`, tracker `--profile highway_runner`.

**Climb the ladder** instead of jumping to the top speed, and score each run
(`python3 analyze_run.py logs/runs/$(ls -t logs/runs | head -1) --follow-seconds 60`):
car 8, 12, 16, 20 m/s; runner 4, 7, 10 m/s. The first rung that fails shows the real limit of the
sim drone. Send `scripts/range_report.py` output for that run.

### Stronger-thrust quad (optional, experimental)

The stock Iris is the right size class (1.5 kg, ~10 inch props) but its thrust-to-weight is only about 2.
A 10-15 inch racing-class quad is 4+. To approximate that:

1. `python3 sim/make_fast_quad.py ~/ardupilot_gazebo --thrust-scale 1.0`, then
   `sim/launch_sim.sh ardupilot highway-fast`. At 1.0 it must fly exactly like the stock Iris.
2. Only then `--thrust-scale 1.4` (~2x thrust). Hover throttle drops; ArduPilot relearns it, so take off twice.

### What the offline model says (fixed camera, assumed 10-15 inch quad, 6 m/s² limit)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| highway sprinter 10 m/s, 100 s | PASS (all frames visible, 100% within ±20%) |
| highway car 20 m/s, 120 s | PASS (97% within ±20%; passes on 4 noise seeds) |

How it gets there: the drone must be allowed to be faster than the target (cap 14 m/s for a 10 m/s
runner, 24 m/s for a 20 m/s car), the follow standoff is closer (8 m altitude) so the lean of the camera
stays inside the frame, and the autopilot acceleration limit (6 m/s²) bounds the lean spikes. At 9 m/s²
the same car case fails (target lost), so **keep `WPNAV_ACCEL` at 600**.

**Not validated:** none of the highway files has been run in Gazebo yet. The thrust scaling in
`make_fast_quad.py` is the least certain part. The 10-15 inch airframe numbers are assumptions; send real
mass, motor and prop data to replace them. No obstacle avoidance; one lane-change option only.

## Highway run 1 findings and fixes (car lost, no re-acquisition, rocking)

**Symptoms (Gazebo):** at 20 m/s the lock lasted a few seconds and was never recovered; the 8 m/s runner
worked but the drone rocked back and forth.

**Reproduced offline.** The model only had single-frame detector misses. Real detectors drop targets in
*bursts* (about 0.3 s). Adding bursts reproduces both symptoms: the 20 m/s car case falls from PASS to
24% visible, and the speed command swings 4-5x more often. Cause: when the target vanished, the forward
command decayed with a 0.15 s half-life, so the drone braked, the car pulled away, and the next frame
asked for full speed again.

**Fixes (all covered by tests):**
1. **Coast through short dropouts** (`tracking.coast_hold_s`, per profile): forward speed is held for up to
   0.2-0.6 s while yaw and climb decay as before. Vehicle profile stays at 0.2 s because longer coasting
   loses cars during lane changes. 20 m/s car with bursts: 24% -> 98% visible.
2. **Re-ID** (`reid.py`, `tracking.reid`): when ByteTrack hands the target a new id, the lock moves to the
   new track if it matches on colour, position (search area widens with time) and size, beats the runner-up
   clearly and holds for 3 frames. It never re-locks to a track that already existed while the target was
   visible, and gives up after `reid.timeout_s` (6 s) by releasing the lock as before. Disable with
   `tracking.reid.enabled: false`.
3. **Steadier speed command** for the fast profiles (`smooth_alpha` 0.6): speed-command swings in the
   model drop from 4-8 per 10 s to about 0 on the runner and highway cases.
4. Run log now also records `gs`, `v_fwd`, `tilt_deg`, `det_ms`, `reid`.

The model now uses 5 frames (0.17 s) of delay plus dropout bursts for every case: 12 of 14 pass on 4
noise seeds (stop-and-go and the 28 m/s car are the two that do not).

**Not proven:** whether your Gazebo drone actually reaches the commanded speed. Check that first:

```
python3 scripts/check_sim_limits.py        # while sim/launch_sim.sh ... highway is running
python3 sim/move_highway.py --target car --car-speed 20 --manual-start    # car waits until you press Enter
python3 tracker.py --source gz:/drone/camera --profile highway             # T, click the car, then Enter in the mover
python3 scripts/pursuit_report.py logs/runs/$(ls -t logs/runs | head -1)
```

`check_sim_limits.py` prints FAIL if the SITL speed limit is still 5 m/s. `pursuit_report.py` shows
ground speed vs commanded speed, dropout bursts, rocking and re-ID events for the run.

**Gimbal.** There is no gimbal drone in the sim. The model says a gimbal adds margin at 20 m/s but is not
what caused these symptoms, so it is not the first fix. ArduPilot's Gazebo repo has an
`iris_with_gimbal` model; it can be added later if a fixed camera still fails after these changes.
